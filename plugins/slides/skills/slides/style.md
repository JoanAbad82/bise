# How a deck should look

A deck is read in a room or skimmed in a hurry. Each slide makes one
point, and the title says it.

## The story first

- Before any script, write the slide titles as a list. Read only the
  titles: they should tell the whole story. If they don't, fix the list,
  not the layout.
- A title is the point, as a short sentence: `Revenue rose 4% on Q2`, not
  `Revenue` or `Q3 overview`.
- One cover slide, the content, one closing slide with the ask or the
  next step. No agenda slide for a deck under 10 slides. No "Thank you",
  no "Questions?" slide unless he asks.
- As few slides as the story needs. Ten slides that each say something
  beat twenty that repeat.

## Text

- At most 5 bullets on a slide, each one line, no full stops after
  fragments. Body text 18 pt or more, titles 28 pt or more; nothing under
  12 pt (sources and footnotes). `check()` flags under 10.
- Plain words and real numbers: `+9% in the North (2 new accounts)`, not
  `strong momentum across key regions`.
- No filler: no "In today's fast-paced world", no "Key takeaways" over
  three lines that repeat the slide, no emoji, no exclamation marks.
- A sub-bullet only when it explains its parent. Never three levels.
- Say where a number comes from, in a small line at the bottom.

## Layout

- Use the deck's layouts and placeholders: the title in the title
  placeholder, the bullets in the body. A text box only for what no
  placeholder holds (a source line, a label on a chart).
- Keep a margin of 48 pt (16:9) on every side; align boxes to the same
  left edge as the title. Nothing touches the edge, nothing overlaps.
- One visual per slide: a chart, a table, or an image, with room around
  it. A chart slide has a title that says what to see and no bullets.
- Tables: 6 rows and 5 columns at most, the header row on, numbers right
  of their label. More data: a sheet, linked in your reply.
- Charts: one series when one is enough; the one that matters in a strong
  color (`#1f6feb`), the others grey (`#afb8c1`). Legend off for a single
  series. Units in the title or the source line.

## Color and type

- The deck's own theme when he sent a deck. In a new deck: dark text
  (`#1f2328`) on white, one accent color, grey for secondary text
  (`#6e7781`). Two fonts at most; the default is fine.
- Bold for a number or a few words that must stand out, not whole
  bullets. No underline for emphasis.
- No shadows, gradients, clip art or decorative shapes. A shape is there
  to group or point at something.

## Before you say done

- `check()` is clean, or each warning is named in your reply.
- Read the titles in order: they tell the story.
- Every slide has one point; no slide is a wall of text.
- Tell the user in a line or two what the deck says and how many slides,
  or what changed, by slide (`slide 3: split in two`).
