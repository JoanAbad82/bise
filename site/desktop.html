why

you didn't become an engineer to babysit robots.

you’ve got more agents than you can keep track of.

five, ten of them at once. you hop from tab to tab all day, and by the evening your brain is soup.

you’d love to hand off more. but letting go is scary.

nobody tells you how to set up agents that run on their own. and when you try, you worry they’ll go off and do the wrong thing, with a token bill to match.

and none of it is fun!

it feels like working the counter at the post office.

what if your team looked like a video game?

bise

so here’s how bise does it.

you talk to one agent, bise. it starts an agent for each job, and only bothers you when something actually needs you.

it’s a simple setup. one lead, one agent per task. the lead figures out who does what, and in which order.

you’re still the boss. you choose the work, and what you want to look at yourself.

and it doesn’t cost more. they’re the same agents you’d run by hand. the lead just keeps them in sync.

:*bise · every project

one agent to talk to.

bise sees all your projects. you say what’s on your mind and it passes the work to the right one. back from lunch? it’ll tell you what happened.

it feels a bit like playing the Sims.

each agent is a bim, a little character in its project’s office. you can see who’s typing, who’s stuck and who’s waiting on you. click one and you’re in its thread.

it only knocks when it needs you.

questions from every project land in one inbox, with one banner, once. you answer from wherever you are, and your answer stays next to the thread it came from.

artifacts

questions you can actually answer.

when ten agents are running, a question like « v1 or v2 for the examples? » shows up with no context. you end up scrolling back through the thread to remember what v1 even was.

so when a decision needs you, the agent writes you a page. it shows what it did and the options side by side. you leave notes right on it, pick one, and send it all back in one go.
